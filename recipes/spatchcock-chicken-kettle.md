# Spatchcock chicken on the Weber

A whole chicken, flattened and roasted with the lid on over a two-zone charcoal fire. It comes out with crisp, golden skin,
juicy meat and a light smoke, and the breast and legs finish at the same time.

- **Serves:** 4
- **Time:** salting (overnight is best, 2 hours at least) · 30 min to light and set up · 45–60 min on the grill · 15 min rest
- **Grill:** Weber charcoal kettle with lid (written for a standard 57 cm kettle)
- **Status:** written 22 October 2026; not yet cooked. Add notes at the bottom.

## Ingredients

**The chicken**
- 1 whole chicken, 1.6–2 kg (free-range if you can; the skin crisps better)
- Fine sea salt: 1% of the chicken's weight (about 18 g for 1.8 kg)
- 1 tsp baking powder (*powder*, not bicarbonate of soda)
- Freshly ground black pepper
- 1 tbsp neutral oil

**Herb butter (optional, but good)**
- 40 g soft butter
- 2 garlic cloves, grated
- Leaves from 3 sprigs thyme, chopped
- Zest of 1 lemon (keep the lemon)

**To finish**
- The zested lemon, halved
- Flaky salt

**Kit:** sharp kitchen scissors · chimney starter · about 70–80 charcoal briquettes (a full chimney) · a foil tray for
drips · probe thermometer · long tongs · heatproof gloves. Optional: a fist-sized chunk of apple or cherry wood for
smoke.

## Method

### Ahead of time

**1. Spatchcock the chicken.** Put it breast-side down. Cut along both sides of the backbone with the scissors and lift
it out (freeze it for stock). Turn the bird over, open it out like a book, and press down hard on the breastbone with the
heel of your hand until it cracks and the chicken lies flat.
*Why:* a whole chicken is uneven, with thick legs and a breast that dries out first. Flattened, it's one even thickness,
so everything finishes together, and it cooks in under an hour instead of an hour and a half. More of the skin faces the
heat, so more of it crisps.

**2. Dry-brine it.** Mix the salt, baking powder and a good grind of pepper. Pat the chicken dry, then sprinkle the mix
evenly over both sides, mostly on the skin. Leave it uncovered on a rack or plate in the fridge **overnight** (12–24 h),
or for at least 2 hours.
*Why:* as with the beef cheeks, salt given time seasons the meat all the way through and helps it hold on to its juices.
Uncovered in the fridge, the skin dries, and dry skin crisps where wet skin steams. The baking powder makes the skin
slightly alkaline, which helps it brown faster and blister into crackly bubbles.

**3. If using the herb butter:** mix the butter, garlic, thyme and lemon zest. Just before cooking, slide your fingers
under the breast skin to loosen it, and push the butter in, spreading it over the breast meat.
*Why:* the breast is the leanest part and the first to dry out. Butter under the skin bastes it from inside as it melts,
and keeps the flavour against the meat. It goes *under* the skin, not on top, so it doesn't stop the skin crisping.

### Setting up the grill

**4. Light a full chimney of briquettes.** Stand the chimney on the charcoal grate, fill it with briquettes, and light
it underneath with a couple of firelighters. Wait **15–20 minutes**, until the top briquettes are covered in grey ash.
*Why:* the chimney lights the coals evenly without lighter fluid, which can leave a chemical taste. Grey ash means the
coals are fully alight and the heat is steady. Put the chicken on too early and the temperature keeps climbing.

**5. Make two zones.** Tip the coals onto **one side** of the charcoal grate and pile them up. Put the foil tray on the
empty side, then put the cooking grate back on. If you're using the wood chunk, put it on the coals now.
*Why:* this is the trick that turns a kettle into an oven. The chicken sits over the empty side, cooked by hot air
moving under the lid, not by flame. The tray catches dripping fat, so it can't flare up and scorch the bird.

**6. Set the vents and heat the grate.** Bottom vent **fully open**. Lid on, with the lid vent **half open** and turned
so it sits over the empty side, opposite the coals. Leave it for 10 minutes to heat up. Aim for **190–200 °C** under the
lid. With no lid thermometer, push the probe through the lid vent for a couple of minutes to read it.
*Why:* air comes in at the bottom, feeds the coals, and leaves through the lid vent. With the vent over the chicken, the
hot air and smoke are drawn across the bird on their way out. More open = hotter; more closed = cooler. Change one vent a
little at a time and give it 10 minutes to settle.

### Cooking

**7. Clean and oil the grate**, then put the chicken on the empty side, **skin-side up, legs pointing towards the coals**.
Rub the skin lightly with the oil. Push the probe into the thickest part of the breast, not touching bone. Lid on.
*Why:* the legs need to reach a higher temperature than the breast, so they face the heat. Oil helps the skin brown
evenly.

**8. Leave it alone for 25 minutes.** Don't lift the lid.
*Why:* every time the lid opens, the heat escapes and it takes several minutes to recover. Cooking with the lid on is
what makes this work.

**9. At 25 minutes, have a look.** If one side is browning faster, turn the bird round, but keep the legs nearer the coals.
Check the temperature under the lid again: if it's dropped below 170 °C (wind does this), open the lid vent a little
more.

**10. It's done when the breast reads 68 °C and the thick part of the thigh reads 78–80 °C,** usually 45–60 minutes in
total.
*Why:* breast meat is juiciest just under 70 °C, and it climbs a couple more degrees while it rests. Thigh meat is
tougher and needs 78–80 °C before it turns tender. Spatchcocking is what lets both reach their right temperature at the
same time.

**11. Optional, for extra crisp skin:** in the last 2–3 minutes, move the chicken skin-side down over the coals. Stay with
it and watch closely; it can go from golden to burnt in under a minute. Grill the lemon halves cut-side down over the
coals at the same time.

### Resting and carving

**12. Rest it for 10–15 minutes**, uncovered or very loosely tented with foil.
*Why:* the juices settle back into the meat instead of running onto the board. Wrapping it tightly would steam the skin
soft again.

**13. Carve.** Cut off the legs and separate the thighs and drumsticks at the joint. Cut each breast away from the bone in
one piece, then slice it thickly across. Squeeze over the grilled lemon and scatter with flaky salt.

## Serving

While the chicken rests, the coals are still hot. Use them:
- **Flatbreads**, a minute or two a side over the coals.
- **Corn on the cob, peppers or courgettes**, straight over the coals.
- **A sauce:** garlic yoghurt (yoghurt, a grated garlic clove, lemon juice, salt) or salsa verde (parsley, capers, mustard,
  vinegar, olive oil).

## Notes

- **Cold or windy days:** the kettle runs cooler, so expect the longer end of the timing. Keep the lid shut and put the
  grill out of the wind if you can.
- **Smaller 47 cm kettle:** use about three-quarters of a chimney.
- **Lumpwood charcoal** burns hotter and faster than briquettes: use about three-quarters of a chimney and check the
  temperature more often.
- **Afterwards:** close both vents fully to put the coals out. Ash stays hot for up to two days, so let it cool completely
  before emptying it.
