# Pumpkin spice syrup (and the latte)

A thick, deeply spiced syrup made with real pumpkin, brown sugar and whole spices. Make a bottle once, then a pumpkin
spice latte takes as long as your coffee does. It's richer and spicier than café versions.

- **Makes:** about 350 ml, enough for 10–12 lattes
- **Time:** 20 minutes to cook · 30 minutes to steep · then 5 minutes per latte
- **Keeps:** 10 days in the fridge; months frozen (see Leftovers)
- **Status:** written 26 October 2026; not yet made. Add notes at the bottom.

## Shopping list

**For the syrup**
- 1 tin pumpkin purée (425 g; you need about 120 g). Libby's is the usual one, in the baking aisle of bigger
  supermarkets. Or see the notes for using butternut squash.
- 100 g light brown soft sugar
- 50 g dark muscovado sugar
- 2 cinnamon sticks
- 4 whole cloves
- 4 allspice berries
- A thumb of fresh ginger (about 3 cm)
- 1 whole nutmeg
- 3 black peppercorns
- Vanilla extract
- Fine salt

**For the lattes**
- Coffee: espresso, or strong coffee from a moka pot, AeroPress or cafetière
- Whole milk (or a "barista" oat milk, which froths properly)
- Optional: whipping cream and ground cinnamon, for the top

**Kit:** a small saucepan · a fine sieve · a clean glass bottle or jar (rinse it with boiling water first) · a milk
frother, or a jar with a lid.

## Timeline

There's no ahead-of-time work here. Make the syrup whenever suits, and it's ready about an hour later. It tastes even
better the next day, once the spices have settled.

## Method: the syrup

**1. Toast the whole spices.** Put the cinnamon sticks, cloves, allspice and peppercorns in the dry saucepan over a
medium heat for 1–2 minutes, shaking the pan, until you can smell them.
*Why:* heat wakes up the oils inside whole spices, and that's where their flavour lives. Toasted spices taste deeper and
warmer; untoasted ones taste flat and dusty.

**2. Add 200 ml water, both sugars and the ginger** (sliced into coins, no need to peel). Bring to a simmer, stirring
until the sugar dissolves, and simmer for 5 minutes.
*Why:* the light brown sugar sweetens; the dark muscovado adds a deep, toffee-like, almost treacly note, which is what
makes this taste richer than a café syrup. Five minutes in hot syrup draws the flavour out of the spices.

**3. Whisk in 120 g of the pumpkin purée** and grate in about a quarter of the nutmeg (½ tsp). Simmer gently for 10
minutes, stirring now and then so it doesn't catch.
*Why:* the pumpkin gives body and a soft, earthy sweetness, and cooking it takes away its raw, vegetable taste. Nutmeg
goes in now rather than at the start because it fades if it's boiled for long.

**4. Take it off the heat and leave it to steep for 30 minutes.**
*Why:* the spices keep giving flavour as it cools. Leave it longer, up to a couple of hours, for a stronger syrup.

**5. Strain it through the fine sieve** into a jug. Press gently with a spoon to get the pumpkin through, but don't
scrape the bottom of the sieve hard.
*Why:* the spices and ginger come out, but most of the pumpkin goes through, and that's what makes the syrup thick and
velvety. Scraping hard pushes through the grainy bits.

**6. Stir in 1 tsp vanilla and a small pinch of salt.** Pour it into the bottle and keep it in the fridge.
*Why:* vanilla rounds the spices off. The salt is too little to taste, but it makes the sweetness taste fuller and the
spices sharper, as it does in salted caramel.

## Method: the latte

**1. Make a double espresso** (or about 100 ml of strong coffee) into your mug.

**2. Stir 2–3 tbsp (30–45 ml) of syrup into the hot coffee.** Shake the bottle first, because the pumpkin settles.
*Why:* syrup dissolves easily in hot coffee but sinks to the bottom of milk. Mixing it into the coffee first means every
sip is flavoured.

**3. Heat and froth about 200 ml of milk** until it's hot but not boiling (around 60–65 °C, when the jug is too hot to
hold for more than a second).
- **With a frother:** as normal.
- **Without one:** heat the milk, pour it into a jar no more than half full, put the lid on tightly, wrap it in a tea
  towel, and shake hard for 30 seconds.
*Why:* past about 70 °C milk starts to taste cooked, and its sweetness drops away.

**4. Pour the milk over the coffee**, holding back the foam with a spoon, then spoon the foam on top.

**5. Optional top:** whipped cream with a spoonful of the syrup whisked into it, then a pinch of cinnamon or a grating of
nutmeg. A few flakes of sea salt on the cream are worth trying: the salty edge against the sweet spice is a lovely
contrast.

**Iced version:** syrup and cold milk stirred together, ice, then a shot of espresso poured over the top.

## Leftovers

- **Fridge:** 10 days, in a sealed bottle. The pumpkin is what limits it. If it smells sour or fizzes, throw it away.
- **Freezer:** pour it into an ice-cube tray. Each cube is about one latte's worth (30–45 ml in most trays), and it melts
  straight into hot coffee. It keeps for 3 months.
- **The rest of the tin of pumpkin:** it freezes well too. Or make a second batch of syrup and freeze it.

**Other uses for the syrup**
- Stirred into **hot chocolate**, **chai** or plain **black tea**.
- Drizzled over **porridge**, **Greek yoghurt**, **pancakes** or **French toast**.
- Whisked into **whipped cream** for any autumn pudding.
- A spoonful in **overnight oats**.
- In a **cocktail**: 50 ml bourbon, 15 ml syrup and 2 dashes of bitters, stirred over ice. A pumpkin old fashioned.
- Brushed over **roasted squash or carrots** in the last 10 minutes as a glaze.

## Notes

- **Stronger:** steep for 2 hours, or add a third cinnamon stick.
- **No whole spices?** UK "mixed spice" is almost the same blend as American pumpkin pie spice. Use 2 tsp of it in place of
  the whole spices and nutmeg, added with the pumpkin. The flavour is a little flatter, and some fine grit settles at the
  bottom of the bottle.
- **No pumpkin?** Roast about 300 g of butternut squash at 200 °C fan until soft (about 40 minutes), then blend it until
  smooth. It's sweeter than tinned pumpkin and works just as well.
- **Spice-only syrup, no pumpkin at all:** follow the same steps without the purée. It's thinner and clearer, and it keeps
  for 3–4 weeks.
