# Chilli miso ramen with crisp chicken and soy eggs

A creamy, deeply savoury chilli miso broth over springy ramen noodles, topped with crisp-skinned lacquered chicken
thigh, jammy soy-marinated eggs, seared pak choi and a knob of butter. Based on a recipe Alexander saw, then worked out
together: a stronger broth, skin-on chicken, a proper egg marinade and real ramen noodles.

- **Serves:** 4
- **Time:** Day 1, about 40 minutes of prep plus overnight marinating · Day 2, about 1 h 15 min
- **Status:** written 1 November 2026; not yet cooked. Add notes at the bottom.

## Shopping list

**Asian supermarket**
- Fresh or frozen ramen noodles, 4 portions (about 130–150 g each)
- Kombu (dried kelp), one 10 cm strip
- Dried shiitake mushrooms, 6
- Doubanjiang (Sichuan chilli bean paste); Pixian or Lee Kum Kee
- White miso (shiro) and red miso (aka)
- Mirin
- Japanese soy sauce (Kikkoman is fine)
- Sichuan chilli crisp (Lao Gan Ma "Spicy Chili Crisp")
- Nori sheets
- Crispy fried shallots (a tub)
- Toasted sesame seeds and toasted sesame oil

**Supermarket or butcher**
- 6 boneless chicken thighs, **skin on** (about 800 g). Supermarket boneless thighs are usually skinless, so ask a butcher,
  or buy bone-in skin-on thighs and cut out the bone (see the notes).
- 6 large eggs (4 for the bowls, 2 spare)
- 1.6 litres fresh chicken stock (pots, not cubes; low-salt if there's a choice)
- 4 baby pak choi
- 2 bunches spring onions
- 3 shallots
- 1 bulb garlic
- 1 large piece fresh ginger (about 8 cm)
- Tahini
- Runny honey
- Caster or granulated sugar
- Butter
- Neutral oil (sunflower or vegetable)

**Kit:** a large pan for the broth · a large frying pan, ideally with a lid · a pot for the eggs and noodles · a bowl of iced
water · a zip-lock bag · a probe thermometer.

## Method

### Day 1: the night before

**1. Cold-soak the stock.** Pour the stock into a jug or the broth pan, add the kombu strip and the dried shiitake, cover,
and refrigerate overnight.
*Why:* kombu and shiitake each carry a different savoury compound, and together they taste far stronger than either
alone. That's the basis of Japanese stock. Soaking them cold draws out their flavour gently; boiling kombu makes it slimy
and bitter.

**2. Marinate the chicken.** In the zip-lock bag, mix 3 tbsp soy, 2 tbsp mirin, 1 tbsp honey, 2 grated garlic cloves, 2 cm
grated ginger and 1 tsp sesame oil. Add the thighs, squeeze out the air, massage, and refrigerate.
*Why:* soy seasons the meat right through; mirin and honey bring sweetness and help it caramelise in the pan, which gives
the lacquered char. Keep it to **4–24 hours**: any longer and the soy starts to cure the meat, which turns firm and
slightly ham-like.

**3. Soft-boil and marinate the eggs.**
- **The marinade:** stir together 100 ml soy, 100 ml mirin, 100 ml water and 1 tbsp sugar until the sugar dissolves.
- **Boil:** bring a pot of water to a full rolling boil. Lower in the eggs straight from the fridge with a spoon, and
  set a timer for **6 minutes 30 seconds**.
- **Chill:** lift them straight into the iced water for 5 minutes.
- **Peel** them under water, starting from the wide end, where there's an air pocket.
- **Marinate:** put them in a small container or a second bag, pour over the marinade, and lay a piece of kitchen paper on
  top so they stay submerged. Refrigerate overnight.

*Why:* 6½ minutes from fridge-cold gives a set white and a jammy, just-runny yolk. The ice stops the cooking at once and
helps the shell come away cleanly. The marinade is diluted with mirin and water, because straight soy over-salts the
eggs and turns the whites rubbery within a few hours. Overnight they turn deep amber and the yolk takes on the seasoning.

### Day 2: the broth

**4. Get everything ready.** Take the eggs and chicken out of the fridge. Lift the kombu out of the stock and discard it.
Squeeze the shiitake back into the stock, cut off their stems, and slice the caps thinly to use as a topping.
- Finely chop the 3 shallots.
- Slice the white parts of 4 spring onions, then thinly slice the greens for garnish.
- Mince 4 garlic cloves and 3 cm ginger.

**5. Fry the aromatics.** In the broth pan, warm 2 tbsp neutral oil over a medium-low heat. Add the shallots and spring
onion whites and cook gently for 5 minutes until soft. Add the garlic and ginger for 1 minute, then **1 tbsp doubanjiang**
(2 tbsp if you like more heat), and fry for 1–2 minutes until the oil turns red.
*Why:* frying the chilli bean paste releases its colour and flavour into the oil, which then carries the heat and
fermented depth all through the broth, rather than only through the chilli crisp on top.

**6. Build the broth.** Pour in the stock, add 2 tbsp soy, 2 tbsp mirin and **2 tbsp tahini**, and whisk until smooth.
Simmer gently for 10 minutes, then turn it down to a bare simmer.
*Why:* the tahini makes the broth creamy and nutty, giving it body without dairy. The bits of shallot and ginger stay in
for texture; strain them out if you'd rather have a perfectly smooth broth.

### Day 2: the chicken and pak choi

**7. Fry the chicken skin-side down.** Take the thighs out of the marinade and pat them very dry with kitchen paper (keep
the marinade out of the pan). Heat 1 tbsp oil in the frying pan over a **medium** heat. Lay the thighs in skin-side down,
press them flat with a spatula for the first minute, and leave them alone for **8–10 minutes** until the skin is deep
golden and crisp.
*Why:* a wet surface steams instead of crisping, and the sugary marinade would burn. Medium heat, not high, because the
honey on the meat darkens fast. Pressing at the start keeps the whole skin in contact with the pan.

**8. Flip, finish and lacquer.** Turn the thighs and cook for 4–5 minutes more, until the thickest part reads **78–80 °C**.
Meanwhile mix the glaze: 1 tbsp soy, 1 tbsp mirin, 1 tsp honey. Brush it over the skin, flip the thighs skin-down for
**15–20 seconds** to set it, then lift them out onto a board, skin up. Rest for 5 minutes.
*Why:* thighs need about 78 °C before they turn properly tender. A few seconds of contact caramelises the glaze into a
lacquer; any longer and it burns. Resting keeps the juices in the meat when you slice it.

**9. Sear the pak choi in the same pan.** Pour off all but 1 tbsp of the fat. Put the pak choi halves in cut-side down and
cook for 2–3 minutes until golden. Add 2 tbsp water, cover with a lid for 1 minute, then lift them out.
*Why:* the chicken fat and browned bits flavour the pak choi. Searing gives the toasty colour; the quick steam softens the
thick stems without charring them.

### Day 2: finishing and assembling

**10. Deglaze the pan.** Pour a ladle of broth into the hot frying pan, scrape up all the browned bits, and tip it all back
into the broth.
*Why:* that browned crust is concentrated chicken flavour; it belongs in the soup.

**11. Add the miso, off the heat.** Take the broth off the heat. In a ladle or small bowl, mix **3 tbsp white miso and 2
tbsp red miso** with some hot broth until smooth, then whisk it back into the pan. Taste: add more soy for salt, more
mirin for sweetness, or more doubanjiang (fried first in a little oil) for heat. Keep it hot, but don't let it boil again.
*Why:* boiling dulls miso's aroma and can make it grainy. Melting it in a ladle first stops it going lumpy. White miso is
sweet and mild; red is deep and salty; together they taste rounder than either.

**12. Cook the noodles.** Bring a large pot of water to the boil, **without salt**. Cook the noodles for 30 seconds less
than the packet says, then drain and shake them hard in the colander. Meanwhile, fill the bowls with boiling water to
warm them.
*Why:* ramen noodles are already salty. Undercooking slightly allows for the time they sit in hot broth. Shaking off the
water keeps the broth from being diluted. Warm bowls keep everything hot to the last spoonful.

**13. Assemble.** Empty the bowls. Slice the chicken into 1 cm batons and halve the eggs (a wet knife cuts the yolk
cleanly). Into each bowl:
- **Broth:** about 375 ml.
- **Noodles:** lift and fold them in so they sit in a neat pile.
- **Toppings:** chicken batons fanned on one side, two egg halves yolk up, a pak choi half, a few slices of shiitake.
- **Butter:** a knob (about 10 g) on top, to melt in.
- **Garnish:** spring onion greens, sesame seeds and crispy shallots.
- **Nori:** a sheet tucked in upright at the edge.
- **Chilli crisp:** a spoonful on top, with more at the table.

*Why the order:* noodles in the broth first so they're coated and warm; the toppings sit on the noodles and stay visible;
nori goes in last because it softens within a minute.

## Leftovers

- **Broth:** keeps 4 days in the fridge, or 3 months frozen. Reheat it gently, without boiling.
- **Eggs:** keep up to 3 days in their marinade. They get firmer and saltier each day. The marinade can be used once more
  for a fresh batch within a week.
- **Chicken:** keeps 3 days. Re-crisp it skin-side down in a dry pan over a medium heat before slicing.
- **Noodles:** never store them in the broth; they swell and go soft. Cook them fresh each time.

**What to turn it into**
1. **Chicken and egg rice bowl:** hot rice, re-crisped chicken, a halved soy egg, pak choi, chilli crisp and spring onion,
   with a few spoonfuls of broth simmered down into a glossy sauce.
2. **Fried rice:** day-old rice fried hard with diced chicken, chopped soy egg, spring onion and a spoon of chilli crisp.
3. **Dumpling soup:** the leftover broth with shop-bought gyoza simmered in it, topped with spring onion and chilli crisp.
   Ten minutes.
4. **Miso loaded fries:** fries topped with crisped chicken, a drizzle of the broth reduced to a sauce, chilli crisp,
   spring onion and Japanese mayonnaise.

## Notes

- **Boning a thigh:** lay it skin-side down. Run a small sharp knife along both sides of the bone, cut underneath it,
  then cut it free at the knuckle. It takes about a minute each.
- **Heat:** 1 tbsp doubanjiang gives a warm background heat, and the chilli crisp adds the rest. For real heat, use 2 tbsp
  and a pinch of ground Sichuan pepper.
- **Smooth broth:** strain it after step 6, before adding the miso.
- **Sweetcorn:** charred corn is the other classic Sapporo topping, left out this time; easy to add later.
