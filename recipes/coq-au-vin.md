# Coq au vin

Chicken braised in red wine and cognac with smoky lardons, finished with goose-fat-roasted mushrooms, carrots and
shallots. Boneless thighs make it calm to cook and easy to reheat; crackling sheets of roasted chicken skin bring back
the crunch, and quick-pickled shallots cut through the richness. Served on mashed potato.

- **Serves:** 6–8
- **Cook early (the plan for 10 November):** braise in the early afternoon, rest, reheat for 7 pm
- **Straight through:** start about 3:30 pm and serve as it finishes
- **Elevated:** braise the day before, refrigerate overnight, reheat on the day
- **Status:** written 9–10 November 2026 with Alexander; first cook 10 November.

## Shopping list

**From the butcher** (ring ahead in the morning for the skin)
- 1.2 kg boneless, skinless chicken thighs (10–12)
- 600 g boneless, skinless chicken breast (3), for anyone who prefers white meat
- 300 g chicken skin, in sheets as large as possible
- 4–6 chicken wings, for the sauce's body
- 300 g smoked lardons, or smoked streaky bacon or pancetta in a piece, cut into thick batons

**Vegetables**
- For the sauce base (fine dice): 2 onions, 2 carrots, 2 celery sticks, 6 garlic cloves
- For roasting (medium dice, about 1.5 cm): 2 carrots, 3 banana shallots, 450 g chestnut mushrooms
- For pickling: 3 banana shallots
- 2 kg floury potatoes (Maris Piper or King Edward)
- A small bunch of flat-leaf parsley (optional, for colour)

**Wine and stock**
- 2 bottles of red Burgundy or another light, fruity Pinot Noir (you'll use about 1.1 litres)
- 75 ml cognac or brandy
- 450 ml good chicken stock (fresh, not cubes)

**Cupboard and fridge**
- Fine sea salt, black pepper, sugar
- 2 tbsp tomato purée
- Goose fat (about 3 tbsp)
- Red wine vinegar (150 ml)
- Fresh thyme, bay leaves, rosemary
- Butter (about 250 g in all) and plain flour (30 g)
- Milk or cream (250 ml), for the mash
- 1 tsp redcurrant jelly (optional)

**Kit:** a large casserole with a lid · a frying pan · 3 baking trays (2 the same size, for pressing the skin) · baking
paper · a probe thermometer · a jar for the pickles.

**Straight through:** the same steps without the rest. Start the skin and lardons at 3:30 pm. Roast the vegetables
while the braise is in the oven, then go straight from step 9 into steps 12–13 as soon as the thighs are tender.
The flavour is a little less settled than when it's rested.

**Elevated:** salt the chicken and braise the day before (steps 1 and 3–9). Cool it, refrigerate overnight, then lift
off the set fat in one piece. On the day, reheat it gently and carry on from step 10. The skin sheets keep for a day in
an airtight box.
*Why resting tastes deeper:* as the braise sits, the wine, cognac and smoke meld into one, and the meat takes back liquid
as it cools. A few hours does much of this; overnight does it fully.

## Method

### In the morning

**1. Salt the chicken.** Weigh the thighs and breast together, and use 1% of that weight in salt (about 18 g). Cut the
breasts into large chunks, about 5 cm. Sprinkle the salt evenly over everything, then keep the thighs and breast chunks
on separate plates, uncovered, in the fridge.
*Why:* hours of salt season the meat right through and dry the surface, so it browns rather than steams. The breast
stays separate because it goes in much later.

**2. Pickle the shallots.** Slice the 3 banana shallots into thin rings and pack them into a jar. Warm 150 ml red wine
vinegar, 75 ml water, 2 tbsp sugar and 1 tsp salt until dissolved, pour it over, and leave it to cool. They're ready in
an hour, and better after several.
*Why:* they're the sharp, fresh note on a very rich plate: sweet-sour, crunchy, and bright pink against the dark sauce.

### The skin sheets

**3. Roast the skin flat.** Scrape any lumps of fat from the underside of the skin and pat it dry. Lay the pieces flat
and stretched on a tray lined with baking paper, and salt them. Rub a little thyme into the salt first, if you like.
Cover with a second sheet of paper and the second tray, and weight it with something ovenproof. Roast at **190 °C fan
for 30–45 minutes** until deep golden and glassy; check from 25 minutes. Drain on kitchen paper, then keep them in an
airtight box once cool. Pour the rendered chicken fat into a jar.
*Why pressed:* left loose, skin curls and shrinks into crinkly bits. Pressed between trays, it sets flat and rigid, so it
can stand up in the mash. It shrinks by about half, which is why you buy 300 g.

### The braise

**4. Render the lardons.** Put them in the cold casserole with 2–3 tbsp water over a medium-low heat. Let the water cook
off and the fat run, then let them crisp. Lift them out, and keep **half** aside for the top.
*Why:* the water melts the fat out evenly before the meat colours, as with the ragù's pancetta. That smoky fat is what
everything else browns in.

**5. Brown the thighs** in the lardon fat over a medium-high heat, in batches with space between them, 3–4 minutes a side,
until deep golden. Pat them dry first. Lift them onto a plate. Then brown the wings the same way.
*Why:* the browning makes the sauce's roasted depth. Crowded meat steams and stays grey.

**6. The base.** Pour off all but 2–3 tbsp fat. Over a medium heat, soften the finely diced onion, carrot and celery with a
pinch of salt for 10 minutes, until soft and lightly golden. Add the garlic for a minute, then the tomato purée, and fry
for 2–3 minutes until brick red.
*Why:* the fine dice melts into the sauce for body and sweetness. Frying the purée caramelises it and takes away its
tinny taste.

**7. Cognac, then wine.** Off the heat, pour in the cognac and scrape the bottom hard. Flambé it if you like: stand back,
light it, and let it burn out by itself. Back on a high heat, add 1.1 litres of the wine and boil it down by a third,
about 15 minutes.
*Why:* the cognac lifts the browned crust and adds warmth. Boiling the wine down drives off the raw alcohol and
concentrates its fruit.

**8. Assemble.** Add the stock, the herbs tied in a bundle, the lardons you didn't keep back, the wings, and the thighs
with their juices. The thighs should be just covered; top up with a little stock or water if not. Bring it to a simmer,
put the lid on, and cook in the oven at **150 °C fan (170 °C conventional)**.
*Why the wings:* their bones and skin give the sauce gelatine, which makes it silky. Boneless thighs alone can't.

**9. Cook for 50–60 minutes, until the thighs are tender** and give easily when pressed (85 °C or more is fine for
thighs). Take the casserole out and leave it on the side with the lid on. It's fine there for up to 2 hours; if your
plan needs longer, let it cool for an hour, then refrigerate it.
*Why:* thighs turn tender as their connective tissue softens, and they're very forgiving. Resting lets the flavours
settle and the fat rise to the top.

### The final hour

**10. Roast the vegetables.** Toss the medium-dice carrots and shallots and the mushrooms (halved or quartered to about
the same size) with 3 tbsp goose fat or the saved chicken fat and a pinch of salt. Spread them in one layer and roast at
**200 °C fan for 25–30 minutes** until golden at the edges.
*Why:* hot, dry oven air browns far better than a crowded pan, and one tray does the lot. They join the sauce only at
the end, so they stay as distinct, roasted pieces rather than stewing.

**11. Reheat the braise** gently on a low hob, stirring now and then, until it's bubbling throughout. Lift out the wings
and herb bundle. Skim off the fat that has risen, keeping a spoonful.

**12. Cook the breast chunks.** Pat them dry and brown them quickly in a hot frying pan with a little fat, 1–2 minutes a
side; they shouldn't cook through. Slip them into the gently bubbling sauce for **10–15 minutes**, until they reach
**68 °C** at the centre. Then stop the cooking.
*Why so late:* breast is lean and turns dry and stringy above 70 °C, so it gets only the time it needs. Browning it
first gives it the same colour and flavour as the thighs.

**13. Finish the sauce.** Stir in the roasted vegetables. Mash 30 g soft butter with 30 g flour into a paste, and whisk it
into the sauce a small piece at a time, letting each piece thicken before adding more. Stop when the sauce coats a
spoon. Taste: black pepper, salt if needed, and the redcurrant jelly if the wine tastes sharp.
*Why:* the butter and flour paste thickens without lumps and gives the sauce a shine; adding it gradually lets you stop
at exactly the right thickness.

## Serving

**Mash:** boil the peeled, quartered potatoes in salted water for 20 minutes until completely soft. Drain, let them
steam dry for 2 minutes, then mash or rice with about 200 g butter and the warm milk or cream. Season well.

**Plating, on warm, wide plates:**
1. **Mash:** a mound on one side, dragged into a crescent with the back of a spoon. Keep it clear of the rim.
2. **Chicken:** a thigh or two (or breast pieces) leaning against the inside of the crescent, half on the mash.
3. **Sauce:** into the open curve, so it pools against the mash rather than flooding it, with the mushrooms, carrots
   and shallots on that side.
4. **On top:** the lardons you kept back over the chicken, then a few pickled shallot rings over the chicken and sauce,
   and a pinch of parsley if you're using it.
5. **The skin, last of all:** one tall shard pushed upright into the back of the mash, and a smaller piece by the
   chicken. Keep it out of the sauce: steam softens it within minutes.
6. **Wipe the rim.** Put a jug of extra sauce on the table.

## Leftovers

- **Keeps** 3 days in the fridge, and it's even better on day two. Keep the skin sheets in an airtight box and
  re-crisp them in a hot oven.
- **What to make:**
  - **Pasta:** shred the chicken into the sauce and toss it through tagliatelle.
  - **A pie:** chicken, sauce and vegetables under a puff-pastry lid, 25 minutes at 200 °C fan.
  - **Loaded fries:** chicken and sauce over fries, with crumbled skin and pickled shallots on top.
  - **Toast:** chicken and mushrooms in sauce on thick toast, with a fried egg and a shard of skin.

## Notes

- **Wine:** Burgundy is the classic, because it's Pinot Noir: light, fruity, and it doesn't turn bitter when reduced.
  Avoid heavy, oaky reds. Serve the same kind with dinner.
- **Showpiece version (skin-on, in a roasting tray):** use bone-in, skin-on thighs and breasts. Make the sauce in the
  casserole without the chicken. Spread the medium-dice vegetables over a deep roasting tray with the thighs on top,
  skin-up, and roast at 210 °C fan for 30 minutes. Then pour the sauce around them, keeping the skin clear, and braise
  uncovered at 150 °C fan. Sear the breasts skin-side in a pan and add them 45 minutes before the end, out at 68 °C;
  thighs out at 80–85 °C. Roast the mushrooms in a separate tray. It's more dramatic, with more timings to juggle, and
  it can't be cooked ahead without the skin softening.
